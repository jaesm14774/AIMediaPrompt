### 春節香煙 — 煙化水墨活山水

廟宇中升起的香煙到了半空突然「活了」——煙霧自動變成流動的水墨山水畫：墨色的山巒在煙中起伏、煙做的瀑布從虛空傾瀉、白煙凝成的仙鶴振翅飛出煙霧邊界飛進真實的廟堂空間。一座在香煙中誕生又消失的山水仙境。

## Prompt Template

A jaw-dropping cinematic photograph of a traditional Chinese temple interior during Lunar New Year, where the rising incense smoke has spontaneously transformed into a **living, animated Chinese ink wash painting (水墨山水畫)** floating in mid-air — the smoke IS the ink, the air IS the rice paper, and the scene painted is a complete miniature immortal realm (仙境) hovering above the worshippers' heads.

**THE SMOKE-INK PAINTING (煙墨山水) — occupying the central 60% of the frame, hovering 2-3 meters above the temple floor:**

The transformation boundary is clearly visible: below, ordinary white-grey incense smoke rises from the bronze censers in natural curling plumes. At approximately head-height, the smoke **changes behavior** — instead of dissipating randomly, the tendrils begin moving with PURPOSE, flowing horizontally and vertically as if guided by an invisible divine painter's brush. The smoke darkens in controlled gradients from pale grey to deep charcoal-black, mimicking the exact tonal range of traditional Chinese ink (墨). What forms is:

[水墨場景 with: 煙霧形成的山水畫內容 / "a complete panoramic mountain landscape (山水全景) rendered in smoke-ink: towering jagged peaks in deep charcoal smoke rise from a base of pale mist-smoke clouds, a waterfall of white smoke cascades down a cliff face with visible liquid-smoke dynamics (the 'water' splashes into a pool that sends tiny smoke-ripples outward), twisted pine trees made of dark grey smoke-wisps cling to cliff edges with individually defined needle-clusters, a tiny arched moon bridge spans a smoke-stream where two microscopic ink-wash style scholars sit fishing — AND HERE IS THE MAGIC: a pair of red-crowned cranes (丹頂鶴) made of white smoke with crimson-glowing crown spots are taking flight FROM the painting, their wings spreading as they cross the boundary between the smoke-painting and the real temple space, transitioning from flat ink-wash style to three-dimensional smoke-sculptures as they emerge, flying toward the temple rafters trailing wisps of smoke-ink behind them like calligraphy brushstrokes dissolving in air" / "a dramatic seascape — enormous smoke-ink waves inspired by Hokusai but rendered in Chinese brush style, curling and crashing in slow motion with spray made of the finest white smoke particles catching the light like liquid silver — riding the largest wave: a miniature Guanyin (觀音) sitting in perfect meditation on a lotus platform made of foam-smoke, completely unbothered by the storm around her, her robes and willow branch flowing with the wave motion — from the sea, a smoke-ink dragon (墨龍) erupts upward, its serpentine body coiling through three successive waves, each scale a tiny whirlpool of dark smoke, its pearl (龍珠) glowing warm gold and serving as the only point of color in the entire monochrome scene — water droplets of smoke fly off the dragon and, as they land on the real bronze censer below, they leave tiny wet-looking ink stains on the metal" / "an intimate garden scene (園林) — a pavilion with curved eaves built from structural smoke so crisp you can see individual roof tile shadows, surrounded by a smoke-bamboo grove where each stalk sways independently in a breeze that only exists INSIDE the painting — a smoke-ink old man like sonar waves in water — where these sound-ripples reach the edge of the smoke-painting and enter real air space, they carry faint wisps of dark ink-smoke that drift down toward the real worshippers below like musical notes made physical"]. The entire smoke-painting is in constant slow motion — mountains shifting imperceptibly, water flowing, clouds drifting, creatures moving — it is alive, being continuously painted and erased and repainted by the smoke itself.

**THE BOUNDARY EFFECT (破界效果):** THIS is what makes the image unforgettable — elements from the smoke-painting are **breaking out** of the painting's boundaries and entering real 3D temple space. [破界元素 with: 從畫中跨越到現實的元素 / "two smoke-cranes have fully exited the painting and are now flying through the real temple interior, their bodies transitioning from flat ink-wash to volumetric 3D smoke-sculpture — one crane passes through a shaft of god-ray sunlight from a high window and its white smoke-body IGNITES with golden luminescence for that brief moment before returning to monochrome as it exits the light beam — the crane's flight path leaves a dissolving calligraphic trail in the air, like a brushstroke written by the bird itself" / "the smoke-dragon's tail extends beyond the painting's edge and coils around a real vermillion temple pillar, the dark ink-smoke wrapping the physical red wood like a living vine — where the smoke touches the pillar, temporary ink-stain patterns appear on the red lacquer surface, fading moments later as the smoke moves on — the dragon's golden pearl casts real warm light on the nearest worshippers' faces" / "smoke-ink waterfall 'overflows' past the painting's bottom edge and the cascade continues downward as real falling smoke, splashing silently into the bronze censer below and causing the incense smoke to briefly swirl in a circular splash pattern — a few smoke-ink fish from the painting's stream tumble over the waterfall edge and swim confused circles in the real air before dissolving back into formless smoke"]. The boundary violations create the sense that the painted world is MORE real than the physical one — the painting is trying to escape into our reality.

**Visual Style:** The real temple environment is shot in warm photorealistic tones (vermillion pillars, bronze censers, red lanterns, golden altar decorations). The smoke-ink painting uses a strictly monochrome Chinese ink-wash palette (pure blacks, greys, whites) with ONLY occasional points of color (crane crown spots in crimson, dragon pearl in gold, falling leaves in faint amber). This color contrast between the warm colorful temple and the elegant monochrome painting is visually striking. The painting should look like a genuine Song Dynasty (宋代) or Yuan Dynasty (元代) masterwork — the style of Fan Kuan (范寬), Ma Yuan (馬遠), or Xia Gui (夏珪) — but made of living smoke. Volumetric lighting from temple windows creates complex interactions where god rays pass THROUGH the smoke-painting, illuminating certain areas and leaving others in shadow, making the painted landscape feel three-dimensional.

**Technical specs:** Shot on medium format, 4:5 or 2:3 portrait, f/4 for deep focus keeping both the smoke-painting details and temple environment sharp, warm-cool split toning (warm temple below, cool ink-monochrome painting above), 8K resolution, the mood is transcendent wonder — witnessing an ancient art form come alive in sacred smoke, the boundary between art and reality dissolving in incense.
